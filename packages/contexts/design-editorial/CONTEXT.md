# Editorial design preset

Optional visual preference profile, not an invented brand or an enforced rule.

The default language is editorial: neutral canvas, one accent, a clear type scale, and spacing that groups related information. Comparable facts can be dense; a consequential decision deserves a quieter layout. Titles favor specific claims; visuals answer an audience question. This profile values source fidelity and readable hierarchy over ornament or repeated card layouts.

Prefer a restrained product interface over a landing-page treatment: moderate title sizes, normal letter spacing, clear labels and breathing room around meaningful groups. Reveal secondary detail through clearly labeled expandable regions, contextual menus or focused dialogs rather than showing everything at once. Keep primary actions, essential status and errors visible; progressive disclosure must not become a hunt for basic functionality. Use inline expansion for nearby detail, menus for secondary actions and dialogs for focused decisions, not nested modal navigation. Dense comparison views and compact mobile travel summaries remain valid when the task calls for them; spacious does not mean oversized or empty.

Aim for clean, minimal structure with richness in typography, composition and useful graphics. Avoid repetitive oversized heroes, slogan-like headings, pill-shaped decoration and cards around every fragment. Travel views prioritize date, local time, route, traveler group and booking state; presentation density follows the material.

Use fine divider lines, deliberate type contrast, aligned columns and whitespace to organize editorial sections without enclosing everything in cards. Full-screen heroes can suit a meaningful introduction or product story; do not treat minimalism as a ban on expressive scale. Keep task-oriented screens direct, avoid repeated hero interruptions and adapt large compositions to mobile, zoom and longer text without clipping.

Motion is quiet and purposeful: subtle appearances, continuity between sections and restrained feedback for an action or state change. It should clarify what appeared, moved or completed without delaying input or reading. Avoid attention-seeking entrances, gratuitous bounce, scroll hijacking and perpetual decorative movement. Use graphics to explain content, not to fill space. Preserve meaning without motion and honor reduced-motion preferences; loading or success must never be communicated by animation alone.

Actual audience, brand references and accessibility needs supersede the preset. Operative checks belong in policy.accessibility.

When selected design context requests a text-scramble hover accent, use it sparingly on short display text. Keep readable text and a stable accessible name as the baseline; hide any decorative scrambled layer from assistive technology. Touch and reduced-motion use must not depend on hover or animation, and the effect must not shift layout or delay actions. Do not scramble body copy, errors or essential action labels.
